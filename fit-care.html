Sock fit and care, made simple

How a sock should fit, how to stop slipping and bunching, and the washing, drying and storage habits that keep every pair comfortable for longer.

Fit

How a sock should fit

A well-fitting sock follows the shape of your foot without pulling or bagging. The heel pocket should sit squarely on your heel and the toe seam should rest at the base of your toes, not across them.

  • Too small: the heel pocket sits under the foot and the sock pulls down over time.
  • Too big: fabric wrinkles under the toes or around the arch, creating friction points.
  • Cuff: should stay up without leaving deep marks on the leg.

Washing

A gentle washing routine

  1. Turn socks inside out and pair them with a sock clip or mesh bag.
  2. Wash at 30–40°C on a gentle or synthetics cycle with mild detergent.
  3. Use a wool-specific detergent for merino and skip bleach entirely.
  4. Leave out fabric softener, especially for microfiber and sports socks.
  5. Wash dark and bright colours separately for the first few washes.

Always follow the care label, as dyes and blends vary.

Drying

Air-dry on a rack or flat. If you must tumble dry, use the lowest heat setting to protect elastane.

Storing

Fold socks flat or roll them loosely. Balling one cuff over the other stretches the elastic over time.

Rotating

Wearing the same pair two days running gives fibres and elastic no time to recover. Rotate several pairs.

Style

Wearing socks well

With tailored trousers, a classic approach is to match socks to the trousers rather than the shoes, which visually lengthens the leg. With casual outfits, socks can be a playful accent: pick up one colour from a shirt or scarf.

  • Sneakers: no-show, ankle or a crisp white crew.
  • Boots: crew or over-calf, slightly thicker for warmth.
  • Dress shoes: thin, fine-gauge socks in navy, charcoal or a muted pattern.
Repair

When to mend and when to retire

Small holes in hand-knitted or wool socks can be darned with matching yarn, giving a favourite pair years of extra life. Thin, worn-through heels on everyday socks are usually a sign it is time to retire them.

Old socks still have uses: dusting cloths, protective covers for shoes in a suitcase, or textile recycling where available.
