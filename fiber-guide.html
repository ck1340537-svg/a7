The Microfiber Bay fiber guide

What your socks are made of changes everything: warmth, softness, drying time and how long they last. Here is a clear look at every common sock fibre.

Synthetics

Microfiber, polyester and nylon

Microfiber is not a single material but a category of extremely fine synthetic filaments, typically under one denier. In socks you will usually find microfiber polyester or nylon, knitted with a small percentage of elastane for stretch and recovery.

Because the filaments are so fine, the fabric has lots of tiny channels that pull moisture away from the skin and spread it out to evaporate. The result is a sock that feels dry, dries quickly after washing and resists abrasion.

  • Strengths: wicking, quick-drying, durable, lightweight.
  • Watch for: can hold odour more than wool; avoid high heat when drying.
Natural plant fibre

Cotton and combed cotton

Cotton is soft, breathable and easy to care for, which is why it dominates everyday socks. Combed and long-staple cottons have shorter fibres removed, giving a smoother, stronger yarn that pills less.

The trade-off is moisture: cotton absorbs a lot of water and dries slowly. For active days, look for cotton blended with polyester or nylon to improve wicking and shape retention.

Animal fibre

Merino wool

Merino sheep produce exceptionally fine wool, which is why merino socks feel soft rather than scratchy. Wool fibres can absorb a good amount of moisture vapour while still feeling dry, and they keep insulating even when damp.

Merino also resists odour naturally, making it a favourite for travel and multi-day hikes. Most merino socks contain nylon and elastane to reinforce heels and toes and to help the sock keep its shape.

Regenerated fibre

Bamboo viscose

“Bamboo” socks are usually made from viscose or lyocell, fibres regenerated from bamboo pulp. The process creates a smooth, silky yarn that feels cool to the touch and drapes softly.

Bamboo viscose is absorbent and pleasant in warm weather, but it is less abrasion resistant than nylon or polyester, so it is often blended for strength. Labels in many countries are required to name it as viscose or rayon from bamboo.

Elastane

Also called spandex or Lycra. Just a few percent gives socks stretch, grip and the ability to spring back into shape.

Nylon

Strong and abrasion resistant. It reinforces heels and toes, the zones that wear first.

Reading the label

Fibres are listed by percentage. The first fibre dominates the feel; the rest tune stretch, strength and drying time.

Construction

Knit details that matter

  • Needle count and gauge: a higher count gives a finer, smoother knit, often found in dress socks.
  • Terry loops: small loops inside the sole add cushioning and trap warm air.
  • Hand-linked or flat toe seams: reduce rubbing over the toes.
  • Arch support bands: tighter knit around the midfoot to stop the sock sliding.
  • Mesh zones: thinner, open knit on the top of the foot for ventilation.
